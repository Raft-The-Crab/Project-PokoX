# 36 — Poko Labs and Community Governance

## Poko Labs

An opt-in area for experimental PokoX features.

### Rules
- Every experiment has a clear purpose and owner.
- Show experimental status before a user enables it.
- Explain known limitations and what data is collected.
- Make participation opt-in and reversible.
- Provide feedback and bug-report controls.
- Do not put critical account, purchase, moderation or child-safety functions behind experiments.
- Use feature flags and staged rollout.
- Maintain rollback capability.
- Do not use experiments to silently change established privacy settings or economy rules.

## Community roadmap governance

- Community members can submit proposals and vote.
- Staff publish status and concise rationale.
- Votes are one input, not binding authority over security, legal compliance, moderation or engineering feasibility.
- Deduplicate similar proposals.
- Rate-limit and detect vote brigading.
- Show when a proposal is merged, deferred or declined.
- Provide a public changelog for accepted proposals.

## Community contribution pathways

- Help answer questions in public spaces.
- Write and review tutorials.
- Translate documentation.
- Test opt-in builds.
- Report reproducible bugs.
- Organize game jams and showcases.
- Mentor new creators.
- Sponsor infrastructure through a transparent, separately managed programme.

## Funding transparency

Before accepting real-money contributions:
- Confirm the responsible legal entity/person and payment provider.
- Publish the purpose, target, deadline if any, fees, refund policy and what happens if the target is not reached.
- Separate restricted project funds from Pokoins and developer payouts.
- Publish periodic totals and expenditure summaries.
- Never promise tax deductibility unless legally established.
- Never imply a donation purchases ownership, staff authority, moderation privileges or preferential discovery.
- Protect donor data and do not publish identities without consent.

## Volunteer safety and governance

- Separate community helpers, moderators, reviewers, QA contributors and infrastructure operators.
- Grant minimum permissions required for each role.
- Require training and probation for sensitive roles.
- Use MFA for privileged accounts.
- Log sensitive access and decisions.
- Review and revoke access promptly.
- Require conflict-of-interest disclosure.
- Do not let volunteers independently control production secrets, sole backups or payment systems.
- Provide a confidential complaint and escalation path.
- Keep child-safety reports restricted to trained, authorized personnel.

## Recognition

- Use descriptive badges and contribution acknowledgements.
- Do not rank volunteers by bans, reports closed or raw enforcement volume.
- Do not imply volunteer recognition is employment or a professional certification.
- Do not make essential community participation dependent on volunteering.

## Acceptance criteria

- Experimental features can be disabled without breaking core functionality.
- Every public roadmap proposal has a visible status.
- Contributions and funds are auditable.
- Volunteer access is role-scoped, logged and revocable.
